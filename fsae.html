Inverter Repackage — Carnegie Mellon Racing (FSAE)

May 2023 System Lead SolidWorks, SimScale, Ansys
Overview

Background

  • The previous generation inverter package was tall, heavy, and difficult to assemble.
  • Design Goals: Reduce vertical footprint and overall system mass, improve heat dissipation, and make assembly in the inverter container secure and easy.

Approach

  • Two 6061 aluminum coldplates (400×324×17mm) cooled in parallel.
  • Used 3D-printed Nylon 12 support standoffs bolted directly to the Inverter Container floor.

Requirements & Constraints

  • Mass: <2000g total system mass.
  • Loading Conditions: Must sustain 10g vertical and 15g lateral loads.
  • Thermal Requirement: Keep IGBT surface temperature below 50°C to prevent thermal throttling.
  • Serviceability: The entire package must be removable using only the bolts that mount to the Inverter Container.
Engineering Analysis

Thermal CFD

  • Assumptions: Modeled constant-power cases at 100W and 150W. Boundary conditions of zero gauge outlet pressure and 35°C ambient temperature. For hand calculations, I assumed that the system was at steady state, there was negligible loss due to radiation, incompressible flow, and the tube was smooth.
  • Results: At the 150W maximum output, the model predicted a peak IGBT temperature of 43.5°C and an outlet water temperature of 36°C.
  • Validation: Performed hand calculations to determine a worst case maximum 3°C coolant temperature increase. CFD showed the actual ΔT was only 1°C, validating the internal flow geometry and justifying that the coldplate would not overwhelm the vehicle's cooling loop.

Structural Analysis

  • Assumptions: Modeled the 4kg system as a rigidly mounted point mass. Assumed linear, isotropic properties for the Aluminum 6061 coldplate and Nylon 12 standoffs. Assumed worst-case equal shear distribution across all four M3 bolts.
  • Failure Mode Analysis: Compression: A 10G vertical load distributed across the ~28mm² combined cross-sectional area of the four M3 standoffs, the average compressive stress is ~14 MPa. Against Aluminum 6061's yield strength of 276 MPa, this yields a Factor of Safety of ~19.7.
  • Failure Mode Analysis: Shear: A 15G lateral acceleration on the 4kg mass distributed across the four M3 bolts resulted in a shear stress of ~20.8 MPa. Compared to a standard Class 8.8 steel bolt's shear yield strength of ~320 MPa, the system has a Factor of Safety > 15.
Results & Future Work

Results

  • Mass Reduction: Achieved a 50% overall mass reduction (1500g) over the previous iteration.
  • Thermal Performance: Reduced the surface temperature differential by 5°C while maintaining steady-state temperatures below the IGBT throttling limits, with a maximum fluid temperature delta of just 1°C.
  • Structural Integrity: Factors of Safety against compression and shear loading conditions of 19.7 and 15, respectively.

Future Work

  • Transient Thermal Modeling: Shift from steady-state CFD to transient simulations to evaluate system performance over a full endurance lap.
  • Dynamic Structural FEA: Analyze vibration and shock loading conditions to more thoroughly assess bolt performance.
  • Pressure Drop Analysis: Perform more robust headloss estimations to accurately predict the coldplate's pressure impact on the overall cooling loop.
